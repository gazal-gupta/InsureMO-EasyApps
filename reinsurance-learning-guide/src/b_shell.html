Follow the risk from insurer to reinsurer

XRI is the InsureMO reinsurance module. It holds every reinsurance contract the company buys (outward) or sells (inward), cedes each policy automatically to the right treaties, places facultative cover, recovers claims from reinsurers, and produces the statements of account that are settled in cash.

Level 0 · Reinsurance business basics

Who cedes what to whom, and why

Reinsurance is insurance for insurers. An insurer (the cedant) passes part of the risks it has accepted to one or more reinsurers, pays them part of the premium, and recovers part of the claims. The policyholder does not see this: the insurer stays fully liable to its clients even if a reinsurer fails.

Why insurers buy reinsurance

Capacity

Write risks bigger than the insurer can carry alone. A 150m refinery can be written by an insurer that only wants to keep 5m.

Catastrophe protection

Limit the damage of one event that hits many policies, for example a typhoon or earthquake. Cat XoL caps the net loss per event.

Stable results

Smooth profit year to year. A quota share reduces the swing from a bad year by sharing every claim.

Capital relief

Reduce the solvency capital required (for example Solvency II, risk-based capital regimes), at the cost of counterparty risk.

Financing growth

Ceding commission from a quota share funds acquisition costs of a growing or new insurer.

Expertise

Reinsurers share pricing, wording and claims know-how for new lines.

Real example · illustrative amounts

A mid-size insurer has capital of 300m and a board rule to lose no more than 5% of capital (15m) in one event. Its typhoon exposure model shows a 1-in-200-year loss of 250m. It buys catastrophe XoL of 235m xs 15m so the worst modelled event costs 15m net, plus the reinstatement premium.

Level 1 · Foundations

What XRI does

XRI consolidates all reinsurance contracts (inward, outward, retrocession; proportional and non-proportional) in one application, cedes business automatically by rules, recovers claims, and produces accounts and settlements. Users are the RI department and other authorised staff.

Key features in the InsureMO XRI guide

  • All contract types digital in one place: inward, outward, retrocession; proportional and non-proportional.
  • Easier renewal with centralised data.
  • Contract structure that mirrors real contracts: multi-currency; sections and subsections by risk class and coverage.
  • Special features: loss participation or corridor, sliding scale commission, profit commission.
  • Mix of quota share and surplus; risk attaching and losses occurring in one contract.
  • Automatic and manual exclusions; special clauses; clean-cut and run-off treaties.
  • Rules-based automatic cession, including mid-term endorsements.
  • Detailed accounting and transaction entries; ready for requirements such as IFRS 17.
  • Combine policies of the same risk; attach overlapping periods or coverage to the right treaties.
  • Adjust cessions after contract binding.
  • Automated settlement and claims recovery.
  • Real-time dashboard, inquiries, statements of account and out-of-the-box reports.

Architecture: insureMO and partner

Split of work: insureMO provides standard APIs and a complete set of app frameworks. The partner owns all BFF (backend-for-frontend) customisation and new features: either a new application calling XRI APIs, or customisation of the provided frameworks. This decides who builds integrations to Easy PA, Claims, Billing and GL in your project.

Where XRI sits

Core concepts

The treaty structure

Level 1

Treaty Level

Shared terms: name, dates, currency, tax, retention base, portfolio type, IFRS 17 condition, financial condition (SOA frequency), warranty, loss participation, event limit, commission, participants (proportional), treaty rules, notes, status history.

Level 2

Section Level

Scope = risk category + section type, for example "property mixed quota-surplus". Claim conditions (notification, cash call, cooperation, control amounts) and the retention / capacity table.

Level 3

Subsection Level

Detailed conditions. Proportional: limits, ceded limit, QS%. Non-proportional: participants, attachment and limit, premium (ROL, MDP), installments, reinstatements.

Facultative facilities have two layers (agreement and subsection). Inward treaties have the same three levels with inward ratio and limit.

Effective dates, versions and slices

Treaty versions

Each adjustment is a modification record with a Modification Effective Date and Mutually Agreed Date. Treaty Status Record keeps the history. Cessions read the version valid on their date.

Policy slices

An endorsement creates a new slice from its effective date. XRI reinsures each endorsement period separately. Cession Adjustment edits only slices on or after the chosen date.

Treaty year

New policies are allocated to the treaty year by policy effective date. Renewal keeps the treaty number, so always report treaty number + year.

Real example · illustrative

A policy runs 1 January to 31 December. On 1 July the sum insured rises from 20m to 25m. XRI keeps slice 1 (Jan–Jun, 20m) and creates slice 2 (Jul–Dec, 25m). If the treaty capacity is 22m, slice 2 needs 3m of facultative cover from 1 July only.

IFRS 17 view of reinsurance held

  • Reinsurance contracts held are measured separately from the underlying insurance contracts.
  • The contractual service margin (CSM) on reinsurance held can be a net cost or a net gain; it is released as services are received.
  • A loss-recovery component recognises the recovery of losses on onerous underlying contracts.
  • Grouping by portfolio, profitability and year matters. XRI’s IFRS17 Condition block (Profit Level, Split Period Type, Portfolio Name) holds data for this grouping.
  • Non-performance risk of the reinsurer is reflected in the measurement.

Certain (public standard). How XRI feeds an IFRS 17 engine is not in the InsureMO XRI guide.
