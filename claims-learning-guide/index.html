Learn insurance, then follow a claim from first notice to final payment

Levels 1 and 2 teach how insurance works across the whole policy lifecycle: parties, principles, underwriting, binding, issuance, endorsement, renewal, cancellation, billing and reinsurance. Levels 3 to 10 go deep into Easy Claim, the InsureMO claims application.

Easy Claim It runs a claim as a chain of tasks: report the loss, register it, investigate, assess the loss, collect documents, calculate, and settle. Each task has a page, a set of buttons and a rule that decides the next task. Approval tasks appear when an amount is above the user's authority.

Policyholder, insured, claimant and payee are often different people

Group health exampleAn employer buys a group health policy. The policyholder is the employer. Each employee is an insured person. Mr Tan (an employee) is admitted to hospital, so he is the claimant. The bill is paid cashless to the hospital, so the payee is the hospital. The TPA handles the medical review for the insurer.
Motor third-party examplePriya's car is hit by a truck. For the truck's insurer, the policyholder and insured is the truck owner. Priya is a third-party claimant: she has no contract with that insurer, but the law gives her a right to claim. If Priya's own insurer pays her first, it becomes a subrogating insurer and claims from the truck's insurer.
Why this matters in Easy Claim. Claimant Information, Sub-Claim (each with a claimant), Payee (on Settlement and Prepay) and Party Management are separate data. One claim can have several claimants and several payees. A tester must test a claim where claimant ≠ payee, and a BA must define who can be a payee for each product.

How an insurer makes money

The pool

Many people pay a small, certain amount (premium) so that the few who suffer a loss get a large, uncertain amount (claim). The insurer manages the pool, predicts the losses with statistics, and invests the money until claims are paid.

Car owners in the pool1,000
Premium each10,000
Premium pool10,000,000
Accidents in the year (4%)40
Average claim160,000
Claims cost6,400,000
Expenses (commission, staff, IT)2,800,000
Underwriting profit800,000

Illustrative numbers. Loss ratio 64%, expense ratio 28%, combined ratio 92%.

Two sources of profit

  • Underwriting result — premium earned minus claims and expenses. A combined ratio below 100% means an underwriting profit.
  • Investment income — premium is collected before claims are paid. The insurer invests that money (the "float"). Long-tail lines such as liability hold claims money for years.

Why this matters for claims staff: every claim paid and every reserve set moves the loss ratio. Accurate reserves let management price the next year correctly; reserves that are too low make the book look profitable until the claims arrive.

Why claims drive insurer profit

Loss ratio Insurance knowledge

Loss ratio = incurred claims ÷ earned premium. Incurred claims = paid claims + change in outstanding reserves. In many non-life lines the loss ratio is between 55% and 80%, so claims are the largest cost of an insurer.

Combined ratio Insurance knowledge

Combined ratio = loss ratio + expense ratio. Below 100% means the insurer makes an underwriting profit. Above 100% means it loses money on underwriting and depends on investment income.

Worked exampleA motor portfolio earns premium of 100 million. Paid claims in the year are 52 million. Outstanding reserves rose from 20 million to 26 million. Incurred claims = 52 + (26 − 20) = 58 million. Loss ratio = 58%. With an expense ratio of 33%, the combined ratio is 91%, so the portfolio made an underwriting profit of 9 million. If claim handlers set reserves 5 million too low, the loss ratio looks like 53% and management makes pricing decisions on wrong data. This is why reserve accuracy matters in Easy Claim.

What is a claim?

    

      
Claim is the whole process that starts when a risk event covered by an insurance contract happens. The insured or beneficiary asks the insurer for payment. The insurer reviews, investigates, assesses the loss, calculates the amount and pays according to the contract terms. InsureMO Easy Claims

      
Easy Claim is a claim application built on the InsureMO Claim API. It gives standard workflows for insurance business and simplifies the claims process. InsureMO Easy Claims

    

    
Why claims matter to the business. Claims are the largest cost line of an insurer. The claim reserve (money set aside for open claims) is a balance-sheet liability, and its accuracy drives reported profit, solvency and reinsurance recoveries. Speed of settlement drives customer retention. Insurance knowledge

Claim mode and sub-claim mode

    

      
Claim mode
One case holds everything
Traditional mode. One claim case contains all information. Used for property insurance, auto insurance and similar lines.

      
Sub-claim mode
One main case, many sub-cases
One main case can contain several sub-cases. Used for health insurance and for multiple claims under one event. Tasks can be created per sub-claim, based on the workflow configuration.

    

    
In both modes a claim has one or more sub-claims. A sub-claim is one damaged object or one person, for example a vehicle, a third-party property or a medical expense. Reserves, loss items, calculation rows and payments are all tied to a sub-claim and a coverage. Sub-claim numbers look like 001; in sub-claim mode the claim number carries the suffix, for example CFHI001202500001155-001. Screenshot

Reserve

    

      
What a reserve is
The insurer's estimate of the expected claim payment. InsureMO Easy Claims

        
TypeMeaning

        
Indemnity reserveExpected payment to the insured for losses.

        
Expense reserveExpenses for handling the claim, such as investigation fees and legal fees.

        
Reserve types seen on screens: Loss (Indemnity), Loss (Expense), Salvage (Expense), Subrogation. Screenshot

      
Reserve operations
  • Update — change the reserve amount.
  • Close — close the reserve.
  • Reopen — reopen a closed reserve.

        
Each operation can need an approval task: Reserve Update Approval, Reserve Close Approval, Reserve Reopen Approval. The Update, Close and Reopen buttons show only for users with the reserve permission set in claim authority configuration.

        
Reserve line columns: Sub-claim, Reserve Type, Coverage Name, Status (Open), Locked, Sum Insured, Outstanding Reserve, Settled. Screenshot

    

    
Reserve vs payment. A reserve is an estimate held on the books. It does not move money. Settlement moves money. When money is paid, the outstanding reserve goes down by the settled amount. Insurance knowledge In BCP, a reserve creates a fee record only (fee type 200201) for the GL, never a receivable or payable. BCP guide

Two kinds of task

    

      
Workflow task
FNOL, Registration, Investigation and the rest of the claim process. Find them in Claim > Claim Task. Each has an owner (a user or the shared pool), a status and a due date. When the due date passes, the work bench highlights the task.

      
Diary task
A reminder task. Create it by hand from the Diary Task link in the case summary, and assign it to a user. Find it in Claim > Diary Task.

    

  


  

    
The authority rule (the key pattern)

    

      
On a working task
When you submit Appraisal, Calculation, Settlement, Prepay, Subrogation or Salvage, the system compares the amount with your authority.
  • Within authority → the next task is created (or the task closes).
  • Above authority → an approval task is created.

      
On an approval task
The approver reads the same page in read-only mode and records a decision.
  • Reject → the original task comes back for rework.
  • Approve, within approver authority → the next task.
  • Approve, above approver authority → a new approval task for the next level.

    

    
This gives multi-level approval without extra screens. Authority amounts are set in claim authority configuration (InsureMO Claim API). See Level 4 for the simulator.

Explained for non-insurance readers Insurance knowledge
How Easy Claim moves a claim
  • Task-based workflow: each stage is a "task" sitting in someone's work queue. Finishing it (Submit) creates the next task automatically, like a relay baton.
  • Approval gates: when an amount is above the user's limit, the system inserts an approval task for a senior person before the claim can move on.
  • Money is separate: Easy Claim decides what to pay; BCP (finance) actually pays. This split is a standard fraud control.
  • Loops are normal: missing documents or a rejected approval send the claim back a step; this is expected, not an error.

Calculation and reserve logic

  
The most technical part of Easy Claim. Two calculation engines exist side by side, and the choice changes how repeat calculations, settlement and reserve closing behave.


  

    
What changed and why

    
Claim requirements grew more complex across product lines, so InsureMO updated the APIs and claim configuration in three areas:

    

      
1. New calculation tool
Replaces the built-in rating function of GIMO. Use it to configure the formulas and logic for claim calculation.

      
2. Claim Matrix V2
A new Claim Matrix version that supports product configuration with the new calculation rules.

      
3. Full-scale calculation
The latest calculation holds the total amount, not the increment. This also allows parallel tasks based on full-scale calculation.

    

    
The update does not change existing functions. Default and existing calculation rules stay the same. Parameters control the behaviour, and tenants who stay on the current version need no extra configuration. InsureMO Easy Claims

Reserve mechanics

    

      
Where reserves are touched
  • Registration — add reserve lines (Add, delete).
  • Investigation, Appraisal — Update, Close, Reopen, Reserve Summary, Reserve History.
  • Case Information — Reserve History and Update.
  • New Subclaim — add reserve for the new sub-claim only; existing reserve is read-only.
  • Approval tasks — Reserve Update / Close / Reopen Approval show Change Amount, New Outstanding Reserve, Settled. Screenshot

      
Reserve arithmetic Insurance knowledge
  • Outstanding reserve = expected total cost − paid so far.
  • Incurred = paid + outstanding. Management and reinsurers watch incurred.
  • A settlement reduces outstanding by the settled amount. A final settlement normally brings it to 0 and closes the reserve.
  • Recoveries (salvage, subrogation) reduce the net incurred cost.
  • Reserve changes above authority need approval because they change the reported liability.

Health insurance baseline

  

    
InsureMO Easy Claims A health insurance claims baseline is available, built on the Claim API and configuration. It adds health-specific fields and rules so the system captures richer data. Health screens exist for FNOL, Sub-Claim Information, Registration, Investigation, Appraisal, Settlement and Case Information.

    
From the screenshots, health FNOL adds: "Claim with other insurer?" (contribution check), "Report to police", Event Description, Agent/Broker Code and Name, Pending Claim toggle, Medical Bill and Payment Info links.

  

  
Insurance knowledge Health claims need data that motor and property do not: diagnosis code (ICD-10), procedure code, hospital (provider) ID, admission and discharge dates, room category, pre-authorisation number, and itemised bills. Regulators in several markets set tight timelines for cashless decisions; for example India's 2024 health master circular asks for cashless authorisation within one hour and final discharge authorisation within three hours. Confirm current rules for your market.

  
Health screenshots in this guide: FNOL · Sub-claim information (on FNOL) · Registration · Investigation · Appraisal · Settlement · Case Information

APIs

    

      
Claim API (claim business)
Easy Claim is built on the InsureMO Claim API. Full API integration lets clients complete a claim within minutes.
https://docs.insuremo.com/tech/pro/sdk/api/claimbusiness

      
ECS API
The claim process supports claim creation by sending the ECS API from the ECS page. Use it for straight-through claim creation from portals or partners.

      
Claim authority configuration
Provided by the Claim API. Controls the reserve buttons (Update, Close, Reopen) and the authority amounts that trigger approval tasks.

      
Batch FNOL
Template per product (sheets: Basic info, Claimant info, Subclaim info, Payee info, Payment Info). Track results in Claim Configuration > Upload Monitor.
