Module 1.1 · Basics

Insurance and claims basics

A claim is the moment the insurer keeps its promise. To design, test or build a claims system, you first need to know where claims sit in the insurance business and why they drive profit.

What a claim is

InsureMO Easy Claims A claim is the entire process where, after a risk event stated in the insurance contract occurs, the insured or beneficiary submits a claim request to the insurer. The insurer then reviews, investigates, assesses the loss, calculates and pays compensation according to the contract terms.

Insurance is risk transfer. Many customers pay a small, certain amount (the premium) so that the few who suffer a loss receive a large, uncertain amount (the claim). The insurer pools the premiums and pays claims out of the pool. Insurance knowledge

The insurance contract (the policy) defines what is covered, up to what amount, for what period and with what exclusions. Claims handling is the process of checking a loss against that contract and paying the right amount to the right person, quickly and with evidence.

Why claims drive insurer profit

Loss ratio Insurance knowledge

Loss ratio = incurred claims ÷ earned premium. Incurred claims = paid claims + change in outstanding reserves. In many non-life lines the loss ratio is between 55% and 80%, so claims are the largest cost of an insurer.

Combined ratio Insurance knowledge

Combined ratio = loss ratio + expense ratio. Below 100% means the insurer makes an underwriting profit. Above 100% means it loses money on underwriting and depends on investment income.

Worked exampleA motor portfolio earns premium of 100 million. Paid claims in the year are 52 million. Outstanding reserves rose from 20 million to 26 million. Incurred claims = 52 + (26 − 20) = 58 million. Loss ratio = 58%. With an expense ratio of 33%, the combined ratio is 91%, so the portfolio made an underwriting profit of 9 million. If claim handlers set reserves 5 million too low, the loss ratio looks like 53% and management makes pricing decisions on wrong data. This is why reserve accuracy matters in InsureMO Easy Claims.

Module 1.2 · Basics

Parties and roles in a claim

A claim involves many parties. Mixing them up is the most common cause of wrong requirements and wrong payee data. Learn who each party is, what they do, and where they appear in InsureMO Easy Claims.

Who talks to whom in a motor claim

Policyholder, insured, claimant and payee are often different people

Group health exampleAn employer buys a group health policy. The policyholder is the employer. Each employee is an insured person. Mr Tan (an employee) is admitted to hospital, so he is the claimant. The bill is paid cashless to the hospital, so the payee is the hospital. The TPA handles the medical review for the insurer.
Motor third-party exampleRiya's car is hit by a truck. For the truck's insurer, the policyholder and insured is the truck owner. Riya is a third-party claimant: she has no contract with that insurer, but the law gives her a right to claim. If Riya's own insurer pays her first, it becomes a subrogating insurer and claims from the truck's insurer.
Why this matters in InsureMO Easy Claims. Claimant Information, Sub-Claim (each with a claimant), Payee (on Settlement and Prepay) and Party Management are separate data. One claim can have several claimants and several payees. A tester must test a claim where claimant ≠ payee, and a BA must define who can be a payee for each product.

How InsureMO Easy Claims stores a claim

Claim (case)

One loss event on one policy. Holds date of loss, report date, cause of loss, catastrophe code, contact details and claimants. Has one Claim No.

Sub-claim

One damage object or risk inside the claim. Fields: Risk Name, Subclaim Type (for example FP-Vehicle), Damage Object, Claim Owner, Claimant Name. Reserves, investigation and loss items sit at sub-claim level.

Reserve line

Sub-claim × Reserve Type (Loss-Indemnity or Loss-Expense) × Coverage. Shows Sum Insured, Outstanding Reserve, Settled, Status (Open / Closed) and Locked.

Calculation and settlement

Calculation lines per reserve type and coverage (Bill Amount, Parameter, Calculated Amount, Adjusted Amount). Settlement lines per payee with Partial/Final, Indemnity Amount, Tax, Ex-Gratia Flag.

Explained for non-insurance readers Insurance knowledge

Claim, sub-claim and reserve in plain words

  • Claim: one folder for one incident on one policy, for example one car accident.
  • Sub-claim: a section inside that folder for each damaged item or each person claiming, for example own car damage and a passenger injury.
  • Reserve: the money the insurer sets aside today for what it expects to pay later. It is an estimate, updated as facts arrive.
  • Why split into sub-claims: different parts move at different speeds and are paid to different people; splitting lets each part close on its own.

Reserves

TypeDescription InsureMO Easy ClaimsIndustry name
Indemnity reserveExpected payment to the insured for lossesCase reserve for loss
Expense reserveExpenses for processing the claim, for example investigation fees and legal feesAllocated loss adjustment expense (ALAE)

Reserve is the insurer's estimate of what the claim will still cost. It is a liability on the balance sheet from the day the claim is registered. Reserve operations in InsureMO Easy Claims are Update, Close and Reopen. InsureMO Easy Claims

Incurred = paid to date + outstanding reserve. When a payment is made, outstanding goes down and paid goes up, so incurred stays the same unless the estimate changes. Insurance knowledge

IBNR (incurred but not reported) covers losses that happened but nobody has reported yet. Actuaries calculate it for the whole portfolio; it is not entered on an individual claim. This is why report date and date of loss are both mandatory: the gap between them feeds the IBNR model.

End-to-end claim lifecycle

InsureMO Easy Claims runs a claim as a chain of tasks. Submitting one task closes it and creates the next. Approval tasks appear only when an amount is above the user's authority. This page shows the whole chain and the task engine behind it.

Standard claim process with approval gates

InsureMO Easy Claims Main chain: FNOL → Registration → Investigation → Appraisal [Appraisal Approval] → Document → Calculation [Calculation Approval] → Settlement [Settlement Approval]. Auxiliary tasks (Prepay, Subrogation, Salvage, Recovery) are created through New Task at any point after registration.

Explained for non-insurance readers Insurance knowledge

How InsureMO Easy Claims moves a claim

  • Task-based workflow: each stage is a "task" sitting in someone's work queue. Finishing it (Submit) creates the next task automatically, like a relay baton.
  • Approval gates: when an amount is above the user's limit, the system inserts an approval task for a senior person before the claim can move on.
  • Money is separate: InsureMO Easy Claims decides what to pay; BCP (finance) actually pays. This split is a standard fraud control.
  • Loops are normal: missing documents or a rejected approval send the claim back a step; this is expected, not an error.

How the task engine works

1. Every Submit creates the next task

After FNOL Submit, the system closes FNOL, creates a Registration task and opens the Task Transfer UI. The next task needs manual completion by a user. InsureMO Easy Claims

2. Task Transfer and Work On

Task Transfer lists pending tasks on the claim. A task checkbox is selectable only if you are the assigned owner or the task is unassigned. Select it and click Work On to open the task page. InsureMO Easy Claims

3. Two ways into a task

From Task Transfer (straight after the previous task) or from Claim Task search: find the claim, then click the edit icon or the Claim No link. The system opens the right page for the task type. InsureMO Easy Claims

4. Workflow tasks and diary tasks

Workflow tasks (FNOL, Registration…) are in Claim Task. Diary tasks are reminders a user creates from the Claim Summary "Diary Task" link and assigns to someone; they are in Diary Task. InsureMO Easy Claims Q9, Q10

A due date is assigned when a task is created. When it expires, the workbench highlights the task. Managers see their team's tasks in Task Monitor. InsureMO Easy Claims Q8, Q12

Tasks and claim owners can be reassigned to a named user or to a shared pool (Reassignment menu), for example when a handler is on leave. InsureMO Easy Claims Q13

Module 2.3 · Core process

Approvals and authority

InsureMO Easy Claims uses one approval pattern for nine task types. Learn the pattern once and you can design, test and configure all of them.

The approval pattern

Trigger

The amount on Submit exceeds the submitting user's authority. Otherwise the next business task is created directly.

Approval page

Same as the original task page, but all claim and sub-claim data is read-only, plus an Approval Decision section (Approve / Reject, Remark).

Escalation

If the approver approves but the amount is above the approver's own authority, the system creates another approval task for the next level.

Explained for non-insurance readers Insurance knowledge

Authority and approvals in plain words

  • Authority limit: the maximum amount a person may approve on their own, set by role (like a spending limit on a company card).
  • Why it exists: it stops one person from paying large or wrong amounts alone, and is checked by auditors and regulators.
  • Escalation: if the approver's own limit is too low, the claim goes one level higher automatically.
  • Reject: sends the work back to the person who submitted it, with a remark explaining why.

Reserves, calculation and settlement money

Reserves carry the financial truth of a claim. This module follows the money from first estimate to final payment, including the new calculation tool, full-scale calculation and the reserve close rule.

Reserve lifecycle

Explained for non-insurance readers Insurance knowledge

Reserves and incurred cost in plain words

  • Reserve: money set aside for a claim that is not yet paid. It is a promise in the accounts, not cash leaving the bank.
  • Paid: money already sent to payees.
  • Incurred: paid plus reserve: the best estimate of the total cost of the claim.
  • Why it matters to the business: profit, pricing and reinsurance all use incurred figures. Wrong reserves mean wrong decisions.

Insurance knowledge Good practice is to set the first reserve on known facts within 24 to 48 hours and review it at every task. Large reserve jumps late in the claim ("reserve creep") distort the loss ratio and are flagged by auditors and reinsurers.

Calculation: two engines

Claim Matrix V1 + GIMO rating InsureMO Easy Claims

The original approach. Calculation uses the built-in rating function of GIMO. Settlement and Prepay screens stay unchanged. Existing tenants need no change.

Claim Matrix V2 + new calculation tool InsureMO Easy Claims

A configuration tool for claim formulas and calculation logic. Products are maintained in Claim Matrix V2. Supports full-scale calculation and parallel tasks. Adds the "Settled" field on Settlement/Prepay, and "Is Reopened" / "Reopened To" in calculation history.

Full-scale versus incremental calculation. InsureMO Easy Claims Old way (incremental): first calculation 1,000; an extra 500 is found; you calculate again with 500 as a new factor; total 1,500 across two records. New way (full-scale): you enter 1,500 in the second calculation and the system treats it as the final total. The latest version always holds the full amount, which is easier to audit and to reverse.

Worked example: Sunrise Textiles fireGross claim USD 1,200,000. Quota share 40% → reinsurer share 480,000, net retained 720,000. A per-risk XOL treaty covers 500,000 xs 250,000 on the retained share → XOL pays min(720,000 − 250,000, 500,000) = 470,000. Net cost to the insurer = 250,000. If salvage brings in 40,000, the recovery is shared the same way (quota share reinsurer gets 16,000 back). Large losses usually trigger a cash call: the insurer asks the reinsurer to pay its share at once instead of waiting for the quarterly account.

Health insurance baseline

InsureMO Easy Claims A health insurance claims baseline is available, built on the Claim API and configuration. It adds health-specific fields and rules so the system captures richer data. Health screens exist for FNOL, Sub-Claim Information, Registration, Investigation, Appraisal, Settlement and Case Information.

From the screenshots, health FNOL adds: "Claim with other insurer?" (contribution check), "Report to police", Event Description, Agent/Broker Code and Name, Pending Claim toggle, Medical Bill and Payment Info links.

Insurance knowledge Health claims need data that motor and property do not: diagnosis code (ICD-10), procedure code, hospital (provider) ID, admission and discharge dates, room category, pre-authorisation number, and itemised bills. Regulators in several markets set tight timelines for cashless decisions; for example India's 2024 health master circular asks for cashless authorisation within one hour and final discharge authorisation within three hours.
