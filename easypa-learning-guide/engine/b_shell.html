Learning guide · basic to advanced

Follow the policy through InsureMO

Easy PA (Policy Administration) is where a policy is born and lives. It records the proposal, prices it, routes it to underwriting, issues the policy, and then handles every change: endorsements, cancellations, undo and renewal. Each step sends data to BCP (money), Reinsurance (XRI) and the Customer module, and Claims depends on the policy it creates.

Built from the InsureMO Easy PA module guide (New Business and Renewal, Endorsement, Common Function, Underwriting, Group Policy, PA Installment Fee, Reinsurance Integration, Policy Authority). Level 0 · Insurance business basics

Who does what in insurance

Insurance is a promise paid for in advance: the client pays premium now, and the insurer pays claims later if a covered event happens. Before the promise is made, someone must sell it, price it and accept the risk. After it is made, it changes, ends or renews. Easy PA records that whole life of the promise.

How underwriting decides

An underwriter asks three questions: how likely is a loss, how big could it be, and is the price right for it? Rules in the product factory handle standard cases automatically; people handle the rest. Domain context

What underwriters look at

  • Travel / personal accident: age, destination, trip length, war or dangerous activities, sum insured.
  • Health: age, medical history (questionnaire), pre-existing conditions, waiting periods.
  • Motor: driver age and history, vehicle value and use, claims history (no-claim discount).
  • Property: construction, fire protection, location (flood), sum insured against treaty capacity.

What they can decide

  • Accept on standard terms (Approve).
  • Accept with a loading (Underwriting Loading +%) or an exclusion clause.
  • Ask for more information or correction (Requote).
  • Refer upwards (Escalate to level 2).
  • Buy reinsurance first (Facultative).
  • Decline (final).

Reinsurance and co-insurance in practice

Treaty

  • Automatic for every policy in a portfolio.
  • Quota share 30%: reinsurer takes 30% of every premium and pays 30% of every claim, plus a ceding commission.
  • Surplus: reinsurer takes the part of each risk above the insurer's retention, up to a limit (capacity).
  • Easy PA sends each risk unit to XRI at bind and issue.

Facultative

  • One risk placed on its own, negotiated each time.
  • Used when SI exceeds treaty capacity or the risk is excluded from the treaty.
  • Example: factory 80 million, capacity 50 million → 30 million placed facultatively.
  • Easy PA: Facultative button → Fac Out → RI response.

Co-insurance

  • Several insurers share a risk directly with the client; no reinsurance contract.
  • The leader issues the policy, collects premium, handles claims, and charges followers a co-fee.
  • Example: leader 60%, follower 40%, co-fee 2% of the follower's premium.
  • Easy PA: Coinsurance Information tab; BCP fee scenarios 3 to 6.

Level 1

Foundations

What Easy PA does, the words it uses, and where it sits in the InsureMO landscape.

What Easy PA does

1. New business and renewal

Captures the proposal, rates it, runs validation and underwriting rules, routes it to underwriters and reinsurance, and issues the policy. Renewal copies an expiring policy into a new proposal.

2. Policy servicing

Endorsements change the issued policy: customer and coverage changes, period changes, cancellation, and undo of the last endorsement. Each one recalculates the premium difference.

3. Common functions

Worklist, sales channel, customer, clause, discount and loading, installments, delivery, documents, notes, versions and print work the same for every product. The source calls these the hardest part to implement.

Why it matters: Easy PA is the source of truth for the policy. BCP bills from its fees, XRI cedes from its risk units, and Claims checks cover against its dates and coverages. A wrong field here becomes a wrong bill, a wrong reinsurance share or a wrong claim decision.

Level 2

Policy lifecycles and status models

Every scenario in Easy PA is a variation of three journeys. Learn the journeys and the status changes, and the scenarios become easy.

The three journeys

New business

Quote → Bound → Issued

  1. Ops team records and saves the proposal (Quote).
  2. Buy runs validation, rating, auto UW and the RI call.
  3. Rule hit → Underwriting (and maybe Fac Out). No hit → Bound.
  4. From Bound: Issue, or Send to BCP → Prepayment → auto-issue.
Endorsement

Quote → Bound → Issued

  1. New Endorsement with type, sub type and effective date.
  2. Change data, Save (Endorsement No.), Calculate (deltas).
  3. UW rule hit → Underwriting; otherwise Bound.
  4. Issue Endorsement. If wrong, Endorsement Undo (last only).
Renewal

Copy → new business

  1. New Proposal → Policy No. → New Renewal.
  2. Data copied; dates one year later.
  3. Any field can change.
  4. Then the new business journey.
Permission rule across statuses: when a case moves to the next status and the same user has authority for it, they can continue. If not, the case goes back to the sharing pool for someone who has.

Easy PA ↔ Reinsurance (XRI)

When Easy PA calls XRI

  1. Underwriting: opening the Reinsurance tab shows treaty information.
  2. Bind: automatic treaty placement.
  3. Issue: automatic final treaty placement.
  4. Facultative: request sent; status Fac Out until the response returns.
  5. Rejection after fac: XRI is told to remove the proposal.

The policy is issued whether or not a treaty matched.

What XRI needs from each policy

  • One risk unit per entry in PolicyRiskList.
  • Risk and coverage: SumInsured, BeforeVatPremium, PolicyStatus; on endorsement also SumInsuredDelta and BeforeVatPremiumDelta.
  • Coverage: ProductElementCode, CoverageName.
  • Risk: InsuredNo (or PolicyElementId), RiskName, RiskLevel (via PARIRiskCategoryMapping).
  • Policyholder with IsPolicyHolder = Y (name, address).
  • Co-insurance: CoInsuranceInfoList with IsLeader and OurShare.

Level 6 · Advanced

Premium and fee logic

How Easy PA calculates premium at four levels, how endorsements and cancellations change it, and which fee records it sends to BCP for accounting.

Premium structure

Per coverage (new business)

  1. AnnualPremium = rated risk premium (formula premium).
  2. GrossPremium = AnnualPremium × POI rate.
  3. Before-VAT premium, first matching case wins: (1) no discount, no adjust → GrossPremium; (2) discount / loading only → GrossPremium × (1 + Σ signed rates); (3) AdjustedPremium set → AdjustedPremium.
  4. Due premium = before-VAT premium + all GST / taxes.

Roll-up and allocation

  • Risk = Σ coverages; PolicyLob = Σ risks; Policy = Σ PolicyLobs (gross, before-VAT and due).
  • An AdjustedPremium at risk, PolicyLob or policy level overrides the sum at that level.
  • The adjustment is allocated back down so each level still sums to the level above.
  • These steps create no BCP fee records; they only fill the premium fields BCP posting reads.

Endorsement calculation in three steps

  1. Calculate new business premium again on the latest policy version (key field AnnualPremium).
  2. Calculate the delta between new and old premium with the POI rate (GrossPremiumDelta, BeforeVatPremiumDelta, DuePremiumDelta).
  3. Add the delta to the prior version to get the new GrossPremium, BeforeVatPremium and DuePremium.

Amount sign and billing modes

Sign

  • + when the insurer receives, − when it pays.
  • New business and endorsement additional premium share the same sign; an endorsement refund reverses it.
  • Commission is − on new business (payable to the channel) and + on refunds (clawback).
  • Co-insurance: the follower's premium is − on the leader's books and + on the follower's; commission and co-fee flow follower → leader.

Billing modes

  • Customer billing (default): own-premium fees billed to the policyholder.
  • Agent billing: own-premium fees billed to the agent / sales channel, who collects from the client.
  • Leader billing (co-insurance follower only): settled through the leader with one consolidated ARAP.
  • Commission always goes to the sales channel, except the follower's co-commission, billed to the other co-insurer. Co-insurance fees always go to the counterparty co-insurer.

Multiple currency, FNLOB and installments

Three currencies

Every fee is produced in transaction, local and booking currency. Local = transaction ÷ transaction→local rate; booking = transaction ÷ transaction→booking rate. Local and booking currencies come from the CurrencyCode table.

Finance LOB (FNLOB)

Coverages can map to different finance LOBs (Product_Finance_LOB_Category), so one fee type can produce several records. ARAP totals, taxes, stamp duty, co-fee and interest are not split. Before-VAT premium and commission are split.

Installments

Single payment or installments (yearly, half-yearly, quarterly, monthly). Periods and due dates come from the POI and plan; per-period amount = total ÷ periods, rounded. Periods can be entered manually.
